### Introduction

**P1.1** In response to the urgent crisis of homelessness on Long Island, the Continuum of Care has developed a five-year Strategic Plan that is ambitious, but possible. This plan prioritizes time-limited and actionable interventions that will produce measurable outcomes on homelessness. Strategic goals and system-level action steps aim to prevent housing loss whenever possible, significantly reduce the experience of unsheltered homelessness, streamline access to emergency shelter and crisis services for those in need, and connect individuals experiencing homelessness with housing opportunities and the necessary support services that meet their specific needs.

**P1.2** Long Island Continuum of Care believes that homelessness is solvable. Still, it will take a "whole of community" approach and the right mix of funding, resources, and partnerships to ensure that every resident in our community has safe and affordable housing.

### About the Long Island Continuum of Care

**P1.3** The Long Island Continuum of Care (CoC) is a local planning body that works to prevent and end homelessness in Nassau and Suffolk Counties. CoC membership is made up of a wide variety of partners, including but not limited to: homeless services organizations, victim service providers, advocates, K-12 school districts, institutions of higher education, faith-based organizations, members of local government, health care providers, public housing authorities, law enforcement, businesses, philanthropists, affordable housing providers, and other social services organizations. CoC membership also includes individuals with lived experience of homelessness, recognizing that those who are closest to the issue often have the strongest solutions. The activities of the CoC are guided by a Governing Board, with committees and subcommittees organized to carry out work related to focus areas, including project ranking, legislative advocacy, coordinated entry, and CoC elections.

**P1.4** The primary function of the CoC is to allocate and implement scarce resources effectively, ensuring that as few people as possible become homeless, and if they do, they quickly return to housing and remain housed.

### Current State of Homelessness

**P1.5** In fiscal year 2024, the CoC assisted 5,447 households experiencing a housing crisis, requiring supportive services, or seeking access to permanent housing. The average episode of homelessness on Long Island lasted an average of 251 days – or almost 7 months. Some households faced additional challenges that prolonged their homelessness. Although families with three or more children make up only 8% of households experiencing homelessness, they remain homeless for the longest period of time - an average of 403 days of homelessness. Single adults over the age of 55 make up 23% of households experiencing homelessness and remain homeless an average of 246 days. ### Key Strategies to Achieve our Vision

**P4.1** This plan sets six strategic goals for 2026-2030 and exists as a "living document", meaning that as the resource landscape and community needs evolve, so will this plan. Action steps for each strategy are developed for the first year of the plan (2026), with additional steps identified by the community for development in years two through five. Future year action steps will be developed based on data-driven analyses of current needs, system performance, and ongoing input from Continuum members. In this way, action steps will build on progress year over year until we reach our six goals.

**P4.2** Two themes emerged in the community input sessions, serving as cross-cutting domains for action steps. We recognize that to achieve our goals and our vision of ending homelessness on Long Island, we must:

1. *Increase housing options that are affordable for people at all income levels.*
2. *Increase awareness and educate our community and legislators about how homelessness impacts our friends and neighbors, and how the entire community thrives when all Long Islanders have a place to call home.*

**P4.3** Therefore, each of the listed strategies includes both a housing and an awareness/education action step.

### Strategic Goal 1: Reduce Unsheltered Homelessness

**P4.4** Between April 2023 and April 2025, the Continuum of Care identified 472 adults living unsheltered on Long Island. Living unsheltered increases health and safety risks, including exposure to extreme weather, vulnerability to physical and sexual violence, and exacerbation of existing health conditions, mental health, and substance use disorders. Additionally, living unsheltered increases the likelihood of contact with law enforcement, and individuals may be ticketed, fined, or jailed for survival actions related to sleeping outside. Lacking a fixed address can also make it extremely difficult for people to meet their daily hygiene needs, receive mail, and find and keep employment.

### Strategic Goal 3: Transform the Shelter System

**P7.1** In Fiscal Year 2024, 92% of households experiencing homelessness utilized the emergency shelter system on Long Island and stayed an average of 261 days in shelter, yet only 25% of these households were recorded as exiting shelter to permanent housing. Providers and individuals utilizing shelter envision a shelter system with clear and understandable eligibility criteria, immediate access to emergency shelter, wrap-around services to support shelter guests in developing life skills and accessing employment opportunities, shelters that facilitate guests' savings for move-out, and support services, such as transportation, that extend beyond the shelter. Both the Nassau County and Suffolk County Departments of Social Services (DSS) are essential partners in addressing this strategic goal. A transformed shelter system offers easy-to-access, safe, and service-oriented emergency shelter options for a diverse range of populations and needs.

### Appendix: Methods

#### Review of System Data

**P12.1** TAC staff conducted a comprehensive review of Long Island's homelessness system data to inform the development of the strategic plan. This included data from the CoC's homeless management information system (HMIS) from 2023-2025, where participant information is recorded at the program level. It also included summary HMIS data, referred to as Longitudinal Systems Analysis (LSA), which utilizes the system's program-level data to create system-level performance data. This data shows how people move through the homeless response system, detailing their points of entry and exit, the duration of homelessness, their exit points, the frequency of returning to homelessness, and whether there are differences in experience across populations. The data review is summarized in the "Current State of Homelessness" section of this strategic plan.

#### Community Input

**P12.2** A total of twelve listening sessions were facilitated by TAC staff during the strategic planning process. Three in-person and two virtual listening sessions were conducted with people currently experiencing homelessness on Long Island. Seven virtual listening sessions were held for service providers and other key partners, including emergency shelter providers in Nassau County and Suffolk County, outreach providers, permanent housing providers, domestic violence service providers, agency leadership from across system partners, and other key community organizations.
